Architectural Framework: The Hybrid Edge-Cloud
Model for Real-Time Performance
The foundational challenge in designing a fully autonomous, real-time AI proctoring
system lies in reconciling the demand for millisecond-level responsiveness with the
computational complexity of multi-modal AI analysis . A purely cloud-centric
architecture introduces network latency that is incompatible with the requirement for
immediate in-session interventions, such as pausing an interview or blocking a
connection . This latency would not only delay critical actions but could also cause
desynchronization between video streams and analytical data, undermining the entire
system's credibility . Consequently, the architectural philosophy must pivot to a Hybrid
Edge-Cloud model, which strategically partitions responsibilities between the client
device and the backend infrastructure to optimize for both speed and scale . This model is
not merely an optimization choice but a non-negotiable prerequisite for achieving the
project's core objective of zero-latency enforcement. The edge component acts as the
frontline sensor and decision-maker, executing latency-sensitive tasks locally, while the
cloud serves as the central brain for high-level aggregation, long-term storage, and
complex analytics .
The client-side, or Edge, architecture is engineered for extreme efficiency and low
latency. Its primary function is to process raw sensory data—video, audio, and user input
—in real-time without relying on a constant, low-latency connection to a remote server.
The specified technologies for this layer include WebAssembly (Wasm) and WebGPU,
which allow for the deployment of highly optimized machine learning models directly
within the browser environment . These frameworks enable near-native code
execution, allowing computationally intensive tasks like computer vision inference to run
efficiently on consumer-grade hardware . The use of quantized ML models, delivered
via TensorFlow.js or ONNX Runtime Web, further reduces the memory footprint and
accelerates inference speed, making it feasible to run multiple simultaneous analyses on a
single device . For instance, lightweight object detection models like YOLOv26 are63

microservices architecture, typically deployed on a Kubernetes cluster that can auto-scale

Identity & Anti-Spoofing Engine: Defeating
Impersonation and Synthetic Media
The most fundamental threat to the integrity of an AI-powered interview is
impersonation, where an unqualified individual substitutes for the legitimate candidate.
The proctoring system must therefore feature an exceptionally robust Identity & Anti-
Spoofing Engine capable of distinguishing a live, bona fide candidate from a wide array
of presentation attacks, including printed photos, video replays, 2D/3D masks, and
increasingly sophisticated deepfakes . Achieving the goal of a >98% detection rate
for spoofing requires a multi-layered defense strategy grounded in established biometric
standards and cutting-edge research in synthetic media forensics . This engine
operates continuously throughout the session, verifying identity passively while also
conducting periodic active checks to thwart injection attacks.13 13

A critical first line of defense is Active Liveness Detection , which involves prompting the
candidate to perform random, unpredictable actions. The proposed method of displaying
a moving dot on a randomized trajectory and asking the candidate to follow it with their
eyes is a powerful technique . This forces the candidate to engage in dynamic micro-
movements and induces a natural pupillary light reflex—the change in pupil diameter in
response to shifts in screen brightness—which is impossible to replicate with a static
image or a simple mask . This form of active verification aligns with the principles
of Presentation Attack Detection (PAD), which focuses on defending biometric systems at
the point of capture (i.e., the camera sensor) . The industry benchmark for evaluating
the efficacy of PAD systems is the ISO/IEC 30107 series of standards . Compliance
with this standard, particularly Level 2 or Level 3, is considered "table stakes" for serious
proctoring solutions, as it ensures the system has been rigorously tested against a diverse
set of spoofing attacks under controlled conditions . These tests yield two key metrics:
the Attack Presentation Classification Error Rate (APCER), which measures the failure to
detect an attack, and the Bona Fide Presentation Classification Error Rate (BPCER),
which measures false rejection of a genuine user . Leading systems aim for
extremely low APCER values, often below 1.0%, demonstrating a high degree of
resistance to known spoofing methods . Certifications from accredited labs like iBeta
serve as a strong indicator of a system's anti-spoofing capability; for example, Shufti
achieved a 0% APCER and 0% BPCER in Level 3 passive liveness testing, while Facia
scored a perfect 0% APCER in Level 2 PAD testing .
While active checks provide strong verification at key moments, continuous passive
liveness detection is essential for ongoing session integrity. This involves analyzing subtle
physiological cues in the live video feed. One of the most promising techniques is the
analysis of remote photoplethysmography (rPPG), which estimates a person's pulse by
detecting minute changes in skin color caused by blood flow in the face . A deepfake
video, even if visually convincing, will almost certainly fail to replicate these real-time
physiological signals, providing a definitive way to identify synthetic media . Another
powerful passive technique is the detection of temporal inconsistencies between audio
and visual signals, a common flaw in lip-sync deepfakes. Research has produced methods
like LIPINC (LIP-syncing detection based on mouth INConsistency) that exploit these
mismatches, achieving detection accuracies of over 95% . By comparing the timing
of lip movements with the corresponding audio waveform, the system can spot fakes
where the mouth shapes are inconsistent with the underlying speech . This multi-
modal approach, integrating audio and video analysis, dramatically increases the
difficulty for attackers, as it requires them to manipulate multiple signal streams
simultaneously and coherently .56144

Behavioral Biometrics Suite: Detecting Cognitive Load
and Anomalous Actions
Beyond verifying identity, the proctoring system must act as a vigilant observer of the
candidate's behavior, as actions are often more revealing than appearances. A
sophisticated suite of behavioral biometrics is essential for inferring cognitive state,
detecting attempts at cheating, and flagging anomalous actions in real-time. This suite
integrates continuous passive monitoring of eye movements, head pose, and hand
gestures with advanced audio analysis to build a holistic profile of the candidate's
engagement and potential misconduct. The goal is to move beyond simple presence
detection to a nuanced understanding of behavior, identifying patterns indicative of
stress, distraction, or collaboration with external agents. The system's ability to
distinguish between normal nervous habits and deliberate cheating maneuvers hinges on
the accuracy and contextual awareness of these behavioral models.
Eye tracking is arguably the most powerful tool in the behavioral suite, as it provides
direct insight into a candidate's focus and thought processes . The system must employ
continuous passive eye tracking to monitor several key metrics: gaze vector, fixation
duration, saccades (rapid eye movements), and pupil dilation . Prolonged off-screen
gazes are a classic indicator of cheating, suggesting the candidate is consulting
unauthorized materials like hidden notes, a secondary screen, or a phone . The
system should be configured to raise a low-level flag if gaze deviates from the designated
area for more than three seconds, triggering a gentle UI prompt before logging a formal
risk event . Conversely, erratic saccades and prolonged fixation on a single point off-
screen can indicate intense concentration on a hidden prompt or script . Head pose
estimation, using models like MediaPipe Pose, complements eye tracking by monitoring
the orientation of the candidate's head in three dimensions (yaw, pitch, roll) . A
sudden turn of the head away from the primary camera feed could signify interaction
with a second person or a hidden device. Some studies have explored simple, efficient
algorithms for head pose detection, focusing on horizontal and vertical orientation to
estimate glances away from the screen . The combination of gaze and head pose
provides a much richer picture of the candidate's attentional focus than either modality
alone.
Hand gesture and pose recognition add another layer of behavioral analysis, enabling the
system to detect interactions with the physical environment. Using skeletal tracking
algorithms like MediaPipe Pose or OpenPose, the system can map the candidate's body
and limbs in real-time . This allows it to detect several high-risk behaviors. For29

Environmental Scanning Module: Securing the
Candidate's Physical Space
To create a truly secure interview environment, the proctoring system must extend its
vigilance beyond the candidate's face and behavior to encompass the entire physical
space captured by the webcam. Unauthorized materials, secondary devices, and covert
communication tools represent significant cheating vectors that must be actively scanned
for and detected in real-time. The Environmental Scanning Module is therefore a critical
component of the system, employing a combination of computer vision, audio analysis,
and advanced sensing technologies to build a complete picture of the candidate's
surroundings. While standard object detection can identify obvious threats, the module
must also incorporate more sophisticated techniques like RF fingerprinting and thermal
imaging to counter advanced, covert cheating methods. This multi-pronged approach
ensures that the system can effectively neutralize attempts to gain an unfair advantage
through environmental manipulation.
The most direct method of environmental scanning is through computer vision-based
object detection. Using a lightweight, edge-deployable model like YOLOv26, the system
can analyze the video feed in real-time to identify and classify objects within the
candidate's field of view . The primary targets for detection would include
prohibited items such as mobile phones, tablets, secondary computer monitors, books,
notebooks, or any other unauthorized materials . The system should not only detect
the presence of these objects but also track their appearance and disappearance over
time, flagging instances where a phone is briefly obscured or moved. Advanced
implementations can analyze reflections in windows or glossy surfaces to detect
secondary screens that might not be directly visible to the main camera . The
integration of a 360-degree environmental scan during the onboarding phase, where the
candidate pans their camera around the room, provides a baseline of the legitimate
environment and helps the system differentiate between authorized and unauthorized
objects . This proactive scanning prevents candidates from introducing cheating aids mid-
interview.
Audio analysis serves as a powerful complementary tool for environmental scanning. As
previously discussed, it can detect the sound of keyboard typing associated with
ghostwriting . However, its utility extends to detecting the presence of electronic
devices through their unique acoustic signatures. Furthermore, advanced audio forensics
can be used to detect secondary persons in the room, whose voices may be too quiet for
the candidate to hear but are easily picked up by the sensitive microphone . The
system should be able to distinguish between the candidate's voice and background noise,12120

Enforcement Protocol: A Deterministic Rule-Based
Intervention System
The user's directive for a "fully autonomous" system with "extremely strict enforcement
protocols" necessitates a robust intervention mechanism that goes beyond the
probabilistic outputs of AI models. While machine learning is essential for detection, the
logic governing actions—warnings, pauses, and terminations—must be explicit,
deterministic, and auditable. The single most common failure in production AI projects is
deploying an agentic AI where a deterministic system was required; therefore, the
enforcement protocol should be architected as a Complex Event Processing (CEP) engine
integrated with a business rules engine, such as Drools . This hybrid approach
allows the system to react intelligently to real-time streams of data by applying a
predefined set of rules that escalate in severity based on the type and confidence of
detected events. This ensures that every action taken by the system is transparent,
repeatable, and aligned with the zero-tolerance policy for integrity breaches.
The core of the enforcement protocol is a tiered system of alerts and interventions,
designed to give the candidate opportunities to correct their behavior before facing
severe consequences. This escalates from low-level flags to critical blocks, creating a
graduated response to cheating attempts. The system must be designed to minimize false12

Post-Session Forensics and Data Governance: Auditable
Integrity and Compliance
A truly comprehensive proctoring system does not end when the interview concludes; its
work is validated and solidified in the post-session forensic analysis phase. This module is
responsible for transforming the raw, real-time telemetry stream into an auditable,
immutable record of the session's integrity. It serves as the ultimate arbiter, allowing
recruiters to validate the system's real-time decisions, uncover subtle incidents that may
have been missed, and generate detailed reports for compliance and accountability
purposes. The design of this module is inextricably linked to the overarching principles of
data governance, which mandate strict adherence to privacy regulations like GDPR, BIPA,
and CCPA, as well as security standards like ISO 27001 and NIST SP 800-53 . A
failure in data governance can render the system legally untenable and ethically
indefensible, regardless of its technical prowess.
The core of the forensic module is the Recruiter Dashboard, which functions as a
command center for reviewing flagged sessions. Its primary feature is a scrub-able video
