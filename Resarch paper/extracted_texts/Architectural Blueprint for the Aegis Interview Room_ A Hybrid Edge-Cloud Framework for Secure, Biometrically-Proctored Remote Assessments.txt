Product Requirements Document (PRD)
This document defines the vision, scope, objectives, and detailed requirements for the
Aegis Interview Room, an advanced AI-powered platform designed to provide a secure,
frictionless, and scientifically validated remote interviewing experience. The primary goal
is to create an environment that is effectively impervious to cheating while ensuring a
positive and fair experience for all candidates. The system leverages a suite of multi-
modal behavioral biometrics, including real-time eye tracking, advanced gesture
recognition, and sophisticated anti-spoofing techniques, to achieve this objective .
The PRD serves as the foundational blueprint for product management, engineering, and
design teams, establishing a clear and unambiguous target for development. It outlines
the system's core capabilities, its operational constraints, and the success metrics against
which it will be evaluated.
The central vision of the Aegis Interview Room is to establish the industry standard for
secure remote assessment by completely neutralizing common cheating vectors, including
impersonation, ghostwriting, unauthorized assistance, and the use of hidden materials 
. This is achieved not through invasive or punitive measures, but through a seamless
integration of intelligent proctoring that respects candidate privacy and maintains a
professional atmosphere . The system's design is predicated on the principle of
transparency; candidates must understand what is being monitored and why, fostering
trust and cooperation rather than suspicion . The ultimate objective is to shift the focus
from manual supervision to intelligent, automated oversight, providing recruiters with
actionable insights that augment their judgment rather than replacing it .
The primary business and technical objectives of the Aegis platform are quantifiable and
ambitious. First, the system must achieve a spoofing attack detection rate of greater than
98%. This includes defeating a range of threats from simple photo attacks to46

The scope of the initial release of the Aegis Interview Room is defined as web-based. The

answer evaluation. The out-of-scope items for this phase explicitly exclude native mobile

maintain tighter control over the browser environment, ensuring that the necessary
technologies like WebRTC and WebGPU are consistently available and behave predictably
across different devices . Other future enhancements, such as automated scoring of
interview responses, are acknowledged as potential Phase 2 features but are not part of
the current development plan .

Technical Requirements Document (TRD)
The Technical Requirements Document (TRD) provides a deep dive into the architectural
decisions, technology stack, and performance specifications required to build the Aegis
Interview Room. This document is intended for the engineering and infrastructure teams,
serving as the definitive guide for implementation. It details the system's logical and
physical structure, the APIs that enable communication between components, and the
rigorous standards governing performance, security, and compliance. The architecture is
deliberately designed to balance the competing demands of low-latency real-time
processing, massive scalability, and stringent data privacy, forming the technical
foundation upon which the entire platform rests.

The cornerstone of the Aegis architecture is a Hybrid Edge-Cloud Model . This design
choice is critical to meeting the system's demanding low-latency requirements for real-
time computer vision tasks . Heavy, latency-sensitive inference operations—such as
facial landmark detection, gaze estimation, and gesture recognition—are executed locally
on the candidate's client device within the browser environment. This client-side
processing is made possible through the use of WebAssembly (Wasm) and WebGPU,
ensuring that computationally intensive tasks do not rely on slow round-trip
communications to a distant server . By processing data at the edge, the system
minimizes network latency, preventing stream desync and providing a responsive user
experience . In contrast, the cloud backend is responsible for orchestrating sessions,
ingesting the high-throughput stream of anonymized telemetry data, performing heavy-
duty analytics and long-term storage, and powering the recruiter's analytics dashboard 
. This division of labor optimizes both performance and cost, leveraging the
computational power of the client device for immediate tasks and the vast scalability of
the cloud for aggregate analysis and archival . The architecture is inherently modular
and forward-compatible, allowing for the independent upgrade of ML models on either
the edge or cloud side without disrupting the entire system .
The client-side, or Edge AI, component is the first line of defense and intelligence. It
relies on several key technologies. WebRTC  is used for capturing high-fidelity, low-
latency audio and video streams directly from the candidate's webcam and microphone 
. For the inference engine, TensorFlow.js  or ONNX Runtime Web  will be
employed to run quantized machine learning models optimized for browser environments
. These models include: a lightweight CNN for Gaze Estimation , based on frameworks
like MPIIGaze or approaches described in recent literature, designed for real-time
performance ; a Pose Estimation  model like MoveNet or MediaPipe Pose to track
body posture ; and a specialized Liveness  model that analyzes pupillary light reflex
changes in response to screen brightness shifts to counter spoofing . The use of ONNX
Runtime is particularly strategic, as it provides a vendor-agnostic, high-performance
inference engine capable of running on various backends, including WebGPU for GPU
acceleration, and supports model formats from multiple training frameworks .
The server-side, or Cloud Backend, is a scalable, containerized environment built on 
Kubernetes  to manage a cluster of microservices . Communication with the backend is
managed through a GraphQL API for flexible data fetching in the frontend application
and RESTful webhooks for event notifications. High-volume, real-time telemetry data
streamed from clients is ingested via an Apache Kafka  cluster, which decouples the data
producers (clients) from the consumers (processing workers) and ensures no data loss
even under peak load . This data is then processed by Python/FastAPI microservices7491

maintain 99.99% uptime to ensure the service is always available .

Security and compliance are woven into every layer of the technical design. The system
must adhere to the strictest standards for biometric data protection, including GDPR,
BIPA, and CCPA . All biometric data, including facial geometry templates derived from
embeddings, must be hashed before being stored in the relational database, with only the
hash value retained to prevent reverse-engineering . Raw biometric data is never stored
in plain text. All data transmission is secured with TLS 1.3, and all video files are
encrypted at rest using AWS S3's server-side encryption with KMS-managed keys (SSE-
KMS) . The system must implement robust anti-spoofing measures, including detecting
Moiré patterns and audio-visual sync drift to thwart video replay attacks, and analyzing
micro-expressions and blood flow via remote photoplethysmography (rPPG) to validate
the presence of a live heartbeat, thereby countering deepfake videos . Access control is
managed through fine-grained IAM roles, and all administrative actions should be logged
in an immutable audit trail to ensure accountability .
Application Flow
The Application Flow delineates the complete, end-to-end journey of a candidate
participating in an Aegis Interview Room session, from initial onboarding to the final
delivery of the integrity report to the recruiter. This flowchart is meticulously designed to
integrate the platform's advanced proctoring capabilities seamlessly into the user
experience, ensuring maximum security without introducing undue friction. The process
is divided into three distinct phases: Pre-Interview Onboarding & Environment
Calibration, Interview Execution & Continuous Monitoring, and Post-Interview &
Reporting. Each phase contains a series of logical steps and checkpoints, with a particular
emphasis on the moments where the AI-driven anti-cheating features are activated and
validated.
Phase 1: Pre-Interview Onboarding & Environment Calibration
This initial phase is critical for setting the stage for a secure and successful interview. Its
primary goals are to authenticate the candidate, verify their equipment, scan their
environment for potential threats, and calibrate the AI models for their specific physical
context.
Authentication:  The process begins when a candidate receives a secure, time-
limited magic link via email. They log in using a Single Sign-On (SSO) provider or a115

Hardware Check:  Upon logging in, the system immediately initiates a hardware
check. It requests permission to access the candidate's webcam and microphone,
and simultaneously runs a background test to measure the available internet upload
speed and network jitter. The system will display warnings if the hardware or

Environment Scan (360°):  To ensure the candidate is alone in a controlled
environment, the system instructs them to slowly pan their camera around the

video feed for the presence of secondary monitors, mobile phones, or other

highlights the item in the candidate's view, asking them to confirm its legitimacy or
remove it from the environment.
Active Liveness Challenge:  Before the main calibration, the system executes an
active liveness check to definitively prove the candidate is a live person. The screen
displays a small, randomly moving dot. The candidate is instructed to follow the
dot with their eyes only, without moving their head. The system analyzes the
resulting eye movements, pupillary response, and subtle 3D depth mapping to
differentiate between a live human and a flat photo, mask, or video replay . This
checkpoint is a crucial defense against spoofing attacks.
Baseline Calibration:  Once liveness is confirmed, the system performs a final
baseline calibration. The candidate is asked to stare straight at the camera for five
seconds. During this period, the system records baseline metrics such as ambient
lighting levels, skin tone characteristics, resting pupil diameter, and the candidate's
natural gaze point relative to the camera lens. This baseline data is later used to
normalize and interpret deviations during the actual interview, improving the
accuracy of anomaly detection .

Backend Schema & Data Architecture
The backend data architecture for the Aegis Interview Room is designed for scale,
performance, and security, employing a polyglot persistence  strategy. This approach
recognizes that different types of data generated by the system have vastly different
characteristics and access patterns, making a single database technology suboptimal.
Relational databases are used for structured metadata, time-series databases for high-
frequency telemetry, object storage for large binary blobs like video, and vector databases
for similarity searches on biometric data . This specialization ensures that each data type
is stored in the most efficient and effective manner possible, supporting the platform's
demanding real-time and analytical workloads while adhering to strict privacy and
compliance requirements.
The relational data, which includes user profiles, session metadata, permissions, and
billing information, is managed by a PostgreSQL  database . The core schema for this
relational store is designed to be normalized yet efficient. The users table stores
candidate, recruiter, and admin profiles, with a crucial column for

facial_embedding_hash . This column stores a cryptographic hash of the candidate's
facial embedding vector, not the vector itself, providing a strong layer of security that
prevents the reconstruction of the original biometric template from the database . The 
sessions  table links candidates to recruiters, tracks the status and timing of each
interview, and stores the final aggregated integrity_score  . To log individual risk
events, a risk_events  table is created. This table is linked to a session and contains a 
timestamp_ms , the event_type  (e.g., 'gaze_offscreen', 'phone_detected'), a 
severity  level, a confidence_score , and a metadata  field in JSONB format. The
JSONB column is ideal for storing the diverse and nested telemetry data associated with
each specific event, such as the exact gaze vector coordinates or the bounding box of a
detected object .
For the high-frequency, time-series data generated by the CV engine—such as gaze pitch/
yaw, head pose, and pupil diameter sampled at 30-60fps per session—the architecture
utilizes a Time-Series Database  like TimescaleDB (an extension for PostgreSQL) or
InfluxDB . This specialized database is optimized for rapid writes and efficient querying
of time-stamped data. The biometric_telemetry  table is configured as a hypertable,
which automatically partitions the data into chunks based on time. This partitioning
dramatically improves query performance when analyzing telemetry for a specific session
or time window, as the database engine can operate on a much smaller subset of the data
rather than scanning the entire table . The schema for this table is straightforward, with a
time column as the primary index, a session_id  foreign key, and columns for each
biometric measurement (e.g., gaze_pitch , head_roll ) . This structure is perfectly
suited for the massive write throughput required during an interview and for the
analytical queries needed to generate heatmaps and other temporal visualizations post-
interview .
Large, unstructured binary data, primarily the recorded video and audio files from
interviews, are stored in a highly durable and scalable Object Storage  system, such as 
AWS S3  . This approach separates the data from the application logic, allowing for easy
backup, versioning, and distribution. Crucially, these S3 buckets are configured to be
private by default. Access to any video file is granted not through direct links but via pre-
signed URLs generated by the backend. These URLs are configured with a very short
expiration time (e.g., 15 minutes), ensuring that even if leaked, they cannot be used to
gain long-term access to sensitive interview recordings . Furthermore, all data written to
S3 is encrypted at rest using server-side encryption with keys managed by AWS Key
Management Service (S3 SSE-KMS), providing an additional layer of security .

system generates a high-dimensional vector representation (embedding) of the
candidate's face during the initial liveness check. This vector captures the unique
geometric features of their face. Instead of storing these vectors in a traditional RDBMS,
they are stored in the vector database, which is optimized for performing fast k-nearest
neighbor (k-NN) searches. When a candidate logs in, their live facial embedding is
compared against the stored baseline embedding to verify their identity. This same vector
database can also be used to detect morphing attacks by comparing the statistical
properties of a live face against known morphed-face patterns .
The data security and encryption strategy is comprehensive and multi-layered. PII
Masking  is applied at the application layer; for instance, candidate names and emails are
encrypted before being inserted into the PostgreSQL database . The principle of Privacy
by Design  is embedded in the architecture, with a strong emphasis on data
minimization . By deploying a hybrid edge-cloud model, the system processes and
analyzes the majority of biometric data locally on the candidate's device. Raw,
identifiable video streams are not uploaded to the cloud; only anonymized telemetry
packets are transmitted, significantly reducing the privacy risk . The data retention
policy is automated and rigorously enforced. Cron jobs or AWS Lambda functions are
scheduled to periodically scan the database and object storage. After a session's data has
served its purpose for review and analytics (typically 90 days), the corresponding entries
in the biometric_telemetry  table, the video files in S3, and the records in the 
risk_events  table are permanently deleted . This automated purging is a key
requirement for demonstrating compliance with regulations like GDPR, which mandate
that personal data should not be kept longer than necessary . For critical
administrative actions, such as a recruiter viewing a flagged session, an immutable audit
log is maintained, potentially using a Merkle Tree pipeline to ensure the integrity of the
log itself .

Phase 4: Adversarial Testing & Beta (Weeks 19-22)  Before public release, the platform

hardened, audited, and beta-tested platform, considered ready for GA.

of the Aegis Interview Room. The final deliverable is the live, fully supported platform.
