Product Requirements Document (PRD)
The Aegis Interview Room is envisioned as the most secure and scientifically validated AI-
powered interview environment, engineered to completely neutralize cheating vectors
such as impersonation, ghostwriting, and unauthorized aid . Its core mission is to
provide recruiters with an intuitive, actionable measure of candidate integrity while
ensuring a frictionless and non-distracting experience for the candidate. This dual-
experience principle is foundational to its design, mandating a separation of concerns
where maximal information is provided to administrators and interviewers, while the
candidate interface remains intentionally minimal to prevent cognitive overload and
trigger false positives in biometric systems . The system's success is predicated on
achieving a >98% detection rate for spoofing attacks, reducing false-positive flags to
<2%, and maintaining 100% compliance with global biometric privacy laws like GDPR
and BIPA .
The scope of the first version of the Aegis platform includes a web-based candidate
interview portal, a real-time computer vision proctoring engine, a recruiter analytics
dashboard, and secure storage for video artifacts . Key functional requirements are
driven by the need for multi-modal behavioral biometrics. FR1 mandates Active Liveness
Detection, where the system prompts candidates with randomized on-screen trajectories
to validate pupillary response and micro-movements, effectively defeating 2D masks, 3D
masks, and photo attacks . FR2 requires Continuous Passive Eye Tracking to
monitor gaze vectors, fixation duration, and saccades, flagging prolonged off-screen
gazes (>3 seconds) or erratic pupil dilation that may indicate reading from hidden
scripts . Advanced Gesture & Pose Recognition (FR3), utilizing skeletal tracking
models like MediaPipe Pose, detects secondary persons in the frame, hand-to-ear gestures
indicative of hidden earpieces, or unauthorized object manipulation . Further,
Environmental & Device Scanning (FR4) uses object detection to identify secondary
monitors or mobile phones, while Audio Forensics (FR5) analyzes background noise to
detect secondary voices or keyboard typing cadence anomalies . All this telemetry14

Technical Requirements Document (TRD)
The technical architecture of the Aegis Interview Room is built upon a Hybrid Edge-Cloud
model, a strategic choice that directly addresses the competing demands of low-latency
processing and high-volume data analysis . Heavy, latency-sensitive Computer Vision
(CV) tasks run locally on the client's browser, leveraging WebAssembly (Wasm) and
WebGPU for accelerated inference. This edge-first approach ensures that real-time
telemetry streams remain responsive and synchronized, preventing the delays that could
frustrate a candidate and degrade the quality of the biometric data being captured .
High-level aggregation, long-term storage, and the complex analytics presented to
recruiters are handled by a scalable cloud backend. This division of labor optimizes both
performance and privacy by minimizing the transmission of raw, sensitive video data to
the cloud .
On the client side, the system utilizes WebRTC for high-fidelity, low-latency audio and
video streaming, forming the foundation of the interview. The local inference engine,
powered by frameworks like TensorFlow.js or ONNX Runtime Web, executes quantized
ML models optimized for browser environments . These include MediaPipe Face Mesh
for 468 facial landmarks, a lightweight MPIIGaze CNN for gaze estimation, and
MediaPipe Pose for 33-keypoint skeletal tracking . A specialized liveness model
tracks pupillary diameter changes in response to randomized screen brightness shifts to
verify a live subject . The server-side cloud architecture is built for scalability and
resilience. An API Gateway using GraphQL provides flexible data fetching for the complex
recruiter dashboard, while REST endpoints handle webhooks and other asynchronous
communications. Real-time telemetry ingestion is managed by Apache Kafka, capable of
handling the massive throughput from WebSocket streams containing biometric data at
up to 60fps . Processing Workers, built with Python and FastAPI, run computationally
intensive algorithms for final report generation and anomaly detection .
Hardware and software requirements are specified for both client and server. Candidate
clients require a minimum 720p webcam, a dual-core CPU equivalent to an i5/Ryzen 5,
8GB RAM, and a modern evergreen browser with WebGPU/WebGL support . Network
stability is crucial, with a minimum 5 Mbps upload speed and jitter below 100ms
recommended . The cloud infrastructure is provisioned on a Kubernetes cluster with
auto-scaling capabilities. GPU nodes, such as NVIDIA A10G instances, are reserved for
fallback heavy inference or post-interview forensic analysis . Video artifacts are stored
securely in AWS S3 buckets, which are configured for private access only, with all content38

Application Flow and Monitoring Logic
The Aegis Interview Room orchestrates a three-phase process: Pre-Interview Onboarding
& Calibration, Interview Execution & Continuous Monitoring, and Post-Interview
Reporting. This flow is meticulously designed to establish a baseline of normal behavior,
vigilantly detect deviations, and present findings in an actionable format. The entire
journey is guided by the asymmetric information principle, where the candidate's path is
streamlined and distraction-free, while the system performs exhaustive background
analysis.
In Phase 1, Pre-Interview Onboarding & Calibration, the system ensures the candidate's
environment and identity are verified before the interview begins. This starts with
authentication via a secure magic link, followed by a mandatory Hardware Check that
verifies the functionality of the webcam, microphone, and internet connection . Next,
an Environment Scan is conducted where the candidate is prompted to pan their camera
around the room; an object detection model like YOLOv8 scans for secondary monitors,
mobile phones, or unauthorized individuals . The cornerstone of this phase is the
Active Liveness Challenge. A moving dot appears on a randomized trajectory, and the102103

UI/UX Design and Asymmetric Experience Architecture
The UI/UX design of the Aegis Interview Room is fundamentally shaped by the
asymmetric information architecture, creating two distinct but complementary user
experiences: a minimalist, distraction-free "Interview Room" for the candidate and a data-
rich, analytical "Command Center" for the administrator and interviewer. This separation
is not merely aesthetic but a core design principle essential for the system's effectiveness
and ethical operation.
The Candidate Interface, or "Interview Room," is governed by the principle of minimal
cognitive load. Complex UI elements can induce erratic eye movements, which would be
incorrectly flagged as suspicious by the gaze-tracking engine, leading to false positives 
. Therefore, the design is exceptionally clean and sparse. The default dark mode
reduces eye strain and enhances contrast for the pupillary tracking algorithms . The
primary screen layout centers the interviewer's video feed or question text, with the
candidate's own small Picture-in-Picture webcam feed in the bottom right . A subtle
top bar displays only essential status indicators: a timer, internet connection strength,
and a discreet "Privacy/Recording" badge. During the initial calibration, a faint, semi-
transparent oval guide helps the candidate position themselves correctly, but this element
disappears entirely once the interview begins to avoid distracting the user . The
system's feedback mechanism is deliberately non-punitive. Instead of a jarring red alert
for minor infractions, a soft, glowing amber border appears around the screen with a
gentle prompt like, "Please keep your face within the frame," giving the candidate a
chance to self-correct before a formal risk event is logged . When an active liveness
check is needed, a smooth, non-intrusive modal slides in, explaining the simple action
required .
In stark contrast, the Recruiter/Admin Interface, the "Command Center," is designed for
rapid, data-driven decision-making. Its layout is divided into panels: a large left panel for
the main video player and a compact right panel for analytics . The centerpiece of the
right panel is a large circular gauge displaying the candidate's final Integrity Score (e.g.,
94/100 - "High Integrity"), providing an immediate, at-a-glance assessment of the
session's integrity . Below the score is a multi-layered telemetry timeline that allows
the recruiter to visualize different data streams simultaneously . Layer 1 might show a
gaze heatmap revealing where the candidate looked most frequently, Layer 2 could
display a graph of head pose angles to identify significant turns away from the screen,
and Layer 3 might highlight spikes in background audio, such as keyboard typing . A
powerful feature is the "Ghost View" toggle, which superimposes the real-time CV data—69

requirements . Both the candidate and recruiter interfaces must adhere to WCAG

This includes using sufficient color contrast, providing text alternatives for non-text
content, and ensuring interactive elements are easy to activate, complying with
guidelines like Success Criterion 2.5.8 (Target Size) .
Backend Data Architecture and Security Schema
The Aegis Interview Room employs a polyglot persistence strategy, recognizing that its
diverse data types—from structured metadata to high-frequency biometric telemetry—
require specialized storage solutions for optimal performance, scalability, and query
efficiency . This approach involves four distinct database technologies working in
concert. Relational data, such as user profiles, session metadata, permissions, and billing
information, is managed by PostgreSQL, chosen for its robust transactional integrity and
ACID compliance . High-velocity, time-stamped biometric telemetry—capturing
data like gaze vectors and head pose at 30-60fps—is stored in a dedicated Time-Series
database, specifically TimescaleDB, which extends PostgreSQL to provide automatic
partitioning and optimized time-based queries . Large binary objects, including full-
resolution video recordings, audio files, and generated reports, are stored in a secure,
encrypted object store, such as AWS S3 . Finally, for identity verification and deepfake
detection, the system uses a Vector Database like Pinecone or Milvus to store facial33

The relational schema for PostgreSQL forms the backbone of the system's metadata. The 
users table stores candidate, recruiter, and admin profiles, linking each user to a
cryptographic hash of their facial embedding rather than the raw vector, a critical
security measure to prevent reverse-engineering . The sessions  table tracks all
interview activities, connecting a candidate and a recruiter, recording the job role, session
status, and the final aggregated integrity_score . The risk_events  table is the
audit log of the proctoring system, storing every flagged incident with a precise
timestamp, event type (e.g., 'gaze_offscreen'), severity level, confidence score, and a
JSONB field for detailed, flexible metadata about the specific event .
The schema for the TimescaleDB hypertable, biometric_telemetry , is optimized for
write throughput. It is partitioned by time, allowing for efficient storage and querying of
millions of data points per session. This structure is essential for running historical
analyses and training machine learning models on long-term behavioral patterns . The
following SQL code illustrates the creation of this hypertable:
-- Telemetry Data (Hypertable)
CREATE TABLE biometric_telemetry (
    time TIMESTAMPTZ NOT NULL,
    session_id UUID NOT NULL,
    gaze_pitch DECIMAL,
    gaze_yaw DECIMAL,
    head_roll DECIMAL,
    head_pitch DECIMAL,
    head_yaw DECIMAL,
    pupil_diameter_mm DECIMAL,
    blink_status BOOLEAN
);
-- Convert to TimescaleDB hypertable for automatic partitioning by time
SELECT create_hypertable('biometric_telemetry', 'time');

only holds a one-way hash, rendering the original data irrecoverable even if the database48

A proactive risk management strategy is embedded in the plan, addressing the most
significant threats to the project's success. The primary risk is a high false positive rate,
which could damage candidate trust and render the system unusable . The mitigation
strategy involves implementing a contextual AI with a "grace period" for minor infractions
and requiring human review for any session with an integrity score below a predefined
threshold (e.g., 80) . The second major risk is client hardware limitations, as the
system's performance depends on the candidate's device . The contingency plan is a
"fallback mode": if WebGPU fails or FPS drops below a critical threshold, the system89
